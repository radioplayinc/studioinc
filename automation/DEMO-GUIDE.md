# Guided demo walkthrough — Studio Sites sales engine

The demo is not a link you drop and hope. You walk the studio owner through
the Amplitude Studios demo live in the thread (email, Instagram DM, or on a
call), one step at a time, so they feel the booking flow as their own client
would. Same engine as the movers demo guide, rebuilt for studios.

Demo link: the `recording-studio-booking-demo` artifact (public link once
shared). Checkout link: the `studio-sites-booking-websites-for-recording-studios`
artifact. Both links live in the tracker once shared.

Rules that never change:
- One message, one step, one question. Send the next instruction only after
  they confirm the step or tell you what they see.
- Plain human prose. No em dashes.
- This is the Presentation stage. It only starts after Transition, when they
  have agreed it makes sense to see what we build.
- Never take payment or card details in chat. The demo collects no card.
  Buying happens only on the Studio Sites checkout page.
- The demo is a static page with no backend in demo mode. A test run saves
  nothing and triggers no alerts or emails. Use clearly test details anyway,
  so nothing ever looks like a real booking.

## Before you send the link
Say what is about to happen and set the test frame:
- This is a working demo of the exact flow their clients would use, running
  on a sample studio.
- They will put in test information and run a pretend booking end to end.
  No real session gets booked, no payment is due, nothing is charged.
- Suggest test details close to their real work so the result feels real: a
  session type they actually sell (a vocal session, a podcast, a mix), their
  own room names in mind, a date about two weeks out.
- The demo shows placeholder rates (Studio A $95/hr, Studio B $65/hr,
  Podcast Suite $55/hr, Mixing $350/song). Tell them to imagine their own
  rates in those slots. Their rates are what the build uses.
- For the contact step, tell them to use clearly fake details, or their own
  if they want to see exactly what their client receives. Either works.

## Walkthrough, booking side (the core demo, every prospect)
Guide them screen by screen, in this order, waiting for them at each step:
1. Loader: tap Enter.
2. "What are you making?" Pick the project type closest to their work.
3. "What do you need?" Recording with engineer, room only, podcast,
   rehearsal, mixing, mastering, or mix + master. Use the test session you
   suggested.
4. Room: Studio A, Studio B, or Podcast Suite. Point out the engineer is
   included in A and the Podcast Suite, and the rate is right on the card.
5. Hours: 2, 4, 8, or 10 hour lockout. Point out longer blocks cost less
   per hour. (Or, on the mixing path: the song stepper. Point out the 10%
   multi-song discount at 4+ songs.)
6. Add-ons: session musician, vocal tuning, stems, rush. Tap one, tap again
   to remove. These are the upsells they currently negotiate by DM.
7. Date and start time. Point out their clients do this part themselves,
   at any hour, without messaging anyone.
8. Contact details: where the estimate and session sheet go.
9. Estimate screen. Let the animated total and the line items land. Ask
   them to notice it is itemized and instant, and the 50% deposit is shown
   right there. This is the moment the sale is made.
10. Deposit screen: pay now or invoice by email or text. Either way the
    time is held.
11. Confirmation: "You are booked in." Booking reference on screen, session
    sheet downloadable. Point out the sheet is what their client walks in
    with.

Then stop talking about the demo and ask one debrief question, NEPQ style.
Good ones:
- "What did that feel like compared to how a client books you now?"
- "If an artist landed on that at 11pm after finding you on Instagram,
  what do you think happens next?"
Let them answer fully. Their words go in the tracker under their key pain
and the demo outcome.

## The tab (every walkthrough ends here)
The demo has a "Need a site like this?" tab pinned to the bottom of every
screen. Point it out at the end: that tab is how their own clients, or any
studio owner who sees the demo, reaches the checkout. If the prospect taps
it during the walkthrough, let them. It opens the Studio Sites checkout.

## If they stall
- If they go quiet mid walkthrough, one nudge only, naming the step they
  were on. Then leave it. The normal 48 hour bump rule applies from there.
- If a screen confuses them, do not defend the design. Note the exact step
  and their words in the tracker and report it to Basqi in the EOD report.
  Confusion in the demo is product feedback.
- If the demo errors for them, stop the walkthrough, note the device and
  step, and tell Basqi the same day. Never claim a fix you have not verified.

## After the walkthrough
- Restate the experience against their stated pain, in their words. Then
  the Commitment questions from the sales engine. Then send the checkout
  link for the package and funding option they chose.
- Log in the tracker: demo done Y/N, which path they ran (room or service),
  their debrief answer in their own words, package interest after the demo.

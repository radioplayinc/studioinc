# The Studio Close
### NEPQ one-call-close script — booking websites for recording studios
*Same engine as The Moving Company Close, rebuilt for studios. Built from
the NEPQ Black Book of Questions. Read top to bottom on a call. Lines in
quotes are word-for-word. Notes in italics are stage direction — tonality,
pauses, what to do.*

**Offer (locked):** Starter $2,499 / Pro $3,499 / Premium $3,999. Deposit is
50% ($1,249 / $1,749 / $1,999). Care plans $70 / $150 / $200 per month by
package, first month free. Timelines: Starter up to 7 days, Pro up to 14,
Premium up to 30 from intake coming back. Guarantee: we fix it until you
are happy, and you do not pay the final balance until you are.
**Demo:** Amplitude Studios demo (booking flow). **Checkout:** Studio Sites

## PART 1 — FIRST CONTACT (email / Instagram DM / text)

**Rule: the first message does not sell. Its only job is to book a
10-minute call or a guided demo walkthrough.**
Keep every message under 3 lines. One idea per message. Sound like a human,
not a blast. Use the prospect's name and one true detail from their site or
Instagram (a room, a genre, a recent post).

### Email opener *(weekday mornings, 9-11am local)*

Subject: quick question about [Studio Name]

> hey [Name], it's Basqi — quick question... when an artist wants to book a
> session at 11pm, who picks up?

*16 words in the body. No pitch, no link. The pain is vivid and specific,
and it is a 3-word answer.*

### Instagram DM opener

> hey [Name], love the room — quick question... when someone DMs you at
> midnight wanting to book, what happens to that message?

### Text opener (only when a mobile is public)

> hey [Name], it's Basqi — quick question... when an artist wants to book a
> session after hours, who picks up?

### Alt opener (rotate to avoid fatigue)

> hey [Name], it's Basqi — do you get a lot of "how much is a session?"
> DMs, or mostly serious bookings?

### When they reply (usually "nobody lol" / "me, eventually" / "my engineer")

> lol yeah that's what I hear from everyone — how many sessions do you
> think slip away like that in a month?

*(Let them answer. Whatever they say: "Ahh, makes sense.")*

> Yeah. The ones who won't wait just DM the next studio. I made a quick
> demo of what a booking page that catches those looks like — want the link,
> or is a quick 10-min call easier so I can walk you through it?

- **Wants the link** → send the demo link, then: `Take a look — if it
  clicks, I've got [Tue 2pm] or [Wed 11am] for a quick walkthrough. Which
  works better?`
- **Wants the call** → `Cool — I've got [time] or [time] tomorrow, which
  one's better? It'll be quick — mostly me asking how you handle bookings
  now, see if there's even a fit.`

### If NO / "we're slammed"

> Ha, good problem to have. I'll leave you alone — but quick question, when
> things slow down... do you ever lose sessions just because you can't get
> to the DM in time?

- **Yes** → back into the sequence above.
- **No** → `No worries — I'll check back in a few months. Good luck out
  there.`

### If "who is this?"

> Basqi — I build booking pages for recording studios. Found your site, just
> curious how you handle it when someone wants to book after hours.

### If "just send me info"

> Yeah for sure — so I send the right thing, what exactly are you looking
> for?

*(Whatever they answer is intel. Then: "Got it — and who do you use now for
bookings?" You are in situation questions now. Steer to a call or a
walkthrough.)*

### No reply? The bump sequence (most replies come from follow-ups, not the opener)

**Day 3 — the bump:**
> hey [Name], not sure if my message came through weird — just had a quick
> q about who picks up when someone wants to book after hours. all good
> either way

**Day 7 — the breakup:**
> no worries [Name], I'll stop bugging you — if the late-night DMs ever get
> annoying, I build booking pages for studios that grab the session while
> you're in the room. -Basqi

*(First time you name what you do. Then drop it — a clean breakup gets more
replies than a fourth nudge.)*

### Booking the call — calendar commitment

> If you have your calendar handy, I can pull out mine and we lock a
> specific time — that way neither of us is chasing the other. Would that
> work for you?

*(If they no-show: wait 2 minutes so it does not look like you were hovering
by the phone, then call: "Hey [Name], it's just Basqi — running a little
behind schedule myself...")*

## PART 1B — THE FOLLOW-UP CALL (they got the message, never replied)

**Call from the same number that texted them, so the thread matches.**
Best windows: 10am-12pm before sessions start, or 4-6pm between sessions.
Never mid-session — mid-session they are tracking (which is the whole
point, see below).

### THE OPENING LINE

> "Hey [Name], it's just Basqi — I sent you a message yesterday about who
> picks up when someone wants to book after hours... figured you were
> probably in a session, so I'd just call quick instead. You got like 30
> seconds — or did I catch you mid-session?"

*Why it works: it is not a cold call, it is a follow-up — the message makes
you familiar. "It's just Basqi" disarms. And the question is a trap in
their favor: if they say "yeah I'm mid-session," they just proved your
point out loud. If they say "I got 30 seconds," you have permission.*

### If they ARE mid-session (expect this a lot — use it)

> "Ha — no, that's literally what I messaged about. I don't wanna be the
> call that costs YOU a session. When are you out of the room today — I can
> ring you at [time] or [time], takes 10 minutes. Which one's better?"

*(Lock the specific time. Add it to your calendar before you hang up. Then
hang up fast — keeping it short when they said they are busy is what makes
them take the callback.)*

### If they have 30 seconds — the bridge into Part 2

> "Cool, I'll be quick. When I message studios that question, most guys
> tell me it goes to the DMs, or voicemail, or just... nobody. What happens
> with you guys when someone wants to book while you're in a session?"

*(Whatever they answer, you are standing at the top of Part 2 Stage 2. Go
straight to the money math: "How many sessions do you think slip away like
that in a month?" → "What's an average session worth?" → let them do the
math. If the pain is real, run Part 2 from there. If you sense they are
getting antsy: "Tell you what — easier if I show you. I can send you the
demo and walk you through it Thursday [time] or Friday [time]?" Book it
either way. Never end without a next step on a calendar.)*

### If they don't pick up — the voicemail drop

> "Hey [Name], it's Basqi — I messaged you yesterday, figured I'd call
> instead. Quick question I ask every studio: when someone wants to book
> after hours, who picks up? If the answer is 'nobody,' that's kind of my
> whole thing — I build booking pages that grab that session while you're
> in the room. I'll send you the demo link after this. Either way, no
> worries. Basqi, [number]."

*(Then immediately send the demo link with: "that was me who just called —
here's the demo I mentioned. If it clicks, I've got [time] or [time] for a
quick walkthrough." Voicemail + message from the same number is the pattern
interrupt — most owners check both on their next break.)*

## PART 2 — THE CALL (one-call close)

**Pre-call checklist:** demo open in a tab, ready to share or send the link.
Their name, studio, and anything from the messages in front of you. Quiet
room.

### STAGE 1 — CONNECTION (first 60 seconds)

> "Hey [Name], it's just Basqi. Thanks for hopping on — I know you're
> probably between sessions, so I'll keep this tight."

> "Quick question before we get into it — when you saw my message about
> the booking site... what was it that made you think 'yeah, I'll give this
> guy 10 minutes'?"

*(Listen. Bridge: "Ahh, got it.")*

> "Cool. So this is pretty basic — mostly I want to understand how you guys
> handle bookings right now... what that's getting you... versus what you'd
> actually want it to look like — see what that gap looks like. And toward
> the end, if you feel like this... might be... what you're looking for, we
> can talk about possible next steps. Would that help you?"

*(This is the status frame. You are the expert diagnosing, not a
salesperson pitching.)*

### STAGE 2 — ENGAGEMENT (85% of the call — live here)

#### Situation questions — fact-finding, 3 to 4 max

> "Walk me through it — right now, when someone wants to book a session
> with you, what does that process look like, start to finish?"

> "How long have you been running it that way?"

> "Where are most of your clients coming from right now — Instagram,
> referrals, Google...?"

> "And in a typical month, how many sessions are you guys doing?"

> "What happens when someone messages while you're mid-session — hands on
> the desk, artist in the booth... what happens to that inquiry?"

*(Bridge every answer: "Ahh." "That makes sense." "Got it." Never stack
questions rapid-fire.)*

#### Problem awareness — find the pain, then make them feel it

> "Do you... like... how the booking side is working for you right now?"

**If "yeah, it's fine":**
> "What do you like about it?"
*(Listen.)*
> "Sounds like it's going 100% perfect... what would you change about it
> if you could, though?"

**Then:**
> "What would you say is the biggest headache with how bookings come in
> right now?"

> "Can you give me a specific example — like the last time that happened?"
> *(precision probing — make them relive it)*

> "How long has that been going on?"

> "Has that had... an impact on you?" *(concerned tone, verbal pace)*

> "In what way, though?"

**The money math — let THEM do it:**
> "Roughly — how many sessions do you think slip through in a month?
> People who reached out and it just... never turned into a booking."

> "And what's an average session worth to you?"

> "So what's the math on that — [X] sessions times $[Y]?" *(let them add
> it up)*

> "So you're leaving around $[Z] on the table... every single month?"

> "Besides you — who else does that hit? You got an engineer waiting on
> work?"

**Seed doubt about competitors:**
> "When someone's scrolling Instagram comparing you to the next studio...
> what do you think makes them DM him instead?"

#### Solution awareness, part 1 — the past

> "Before we started talking today, were you out there looking for a way
> to take bookings online — so you could stop losing those after-hours
> sessions — or what were you doing about it?"

**If no:** > "What's prevented you from doing that in the past?" *(softly)*

**If yes / tried something:** > "What did you try?" → "How'd that work
out?" → "What do you think held you back from getting the result you
wanted?"

**Criteria sequence — this tells you exactly how to present later:**
> "Let's do this — just to make sure what I do could actually help you...
> besides it catching bookings while you're in a session, and [their
> words]... what would you actually be looking for in something like this?"

> "Like what would your ideal setup look like?"

> "Can I ask why that's important to you... now, though?"

> "Anything else?"

#### Solution awareness, part 2 — the future (objective state)

> "Okay, so let's say we get this live for you — it's midnight, an artist
> books their session step-by-step on their phone, picks the room and the
> hours, pays the deposit, and you wake up and it's on your calendar...
> what would that do for you personally?" *(concerned tone)*

> "How would things be different — you not having to stop mid-session to
> answer a DM, no more 'how much is a session?' ten times a day?"

> "I mean, it's not gonna be much — but even 2 or 3 extra booked sessions
> a month... what would that mean for you?" *(mismatching: downplay it, let
> them inflate it)*

#### Consequence questions — rip the future away (challenging/concerned tone, be tougher here)

> "Okay, but on the flip side... what happens if you don't do anything
> about this? You keep running bookings the same way for the next 6, 12
> months... the studios with online booking keep grabbing those evening
> sessions... what happens at that point?"

> "Do you want to keep... losing those sessions... if you... didn't have
> to?"

> "So why look at fixing this now? Why not just push it down the road like
> a lot of guys do?"

**Qualifying — check urgency before you present:**
> "How important is it for you to change this? ... Why now, though?"

*(If urgency is weak, do NOT present. Go back to problem awareness.
Presenting to an unmotivated prospect is how you hear "I need to think
about it.")*

### STAGE 3 — TRANSITION

> "Based on what you told me... what I do could actually work for you.
> Because you said you wanted [their words — e.g. bookings without
> answering DMs at midnight]... and because of that, it's making you feel...
> I think you said frustrated... stretched thin sometimes."

### STAGE 4 — PRESENTATION (10% — show, don't pitch)

**Permission first:**
> "With your permission, I can go over a few things we'd set up for you to
> solve [their 2–3 problems]. Would that help you?"

**The 3-step formula — only their problems, nothing else:**

> **Step 1 —** "One of the biggest problems studios have when they come to
> me is they're losing sessions they never even hear about — someone finds
> them at 11pm, nobody picks up, they DM the next studio. Which, for you,
> is costing around $[their math] a month."

> **Step 2 —** "So the way we solve that is we build you a step-by-step
> booking page — client picks the project, the room, the hours, sees the
> price live, pays the deposit — it lands straight in your calendar, and
> they walk in with a session sheet. No DM tag, no missed midnight
> inquiries." *(Walk the demo live — one booking in 60 seconds, per
> DEMO-GUIDE.md.)*

> **Step 3 —** "And what that means for you is... you wake up to booked
> sessions instead of missed DMs — and next to every other studio on
> Instagram, you look like the professional outfit."

**Checking-for-agreement questions — ask 8 to 12 throughout:**
"Does that make sense?" / "Are we on the same page?" / "Do you see how
that'd plug into how you guys work?" / "Can you see your artists using
that?" / "How do you see that helping you the most?"

**The 3 options** *(middle one is your core offer — the premium makes it
look like the bargain):*

> "There's three ways we can do this — most guys go with the middle one:
>
> **Option 1 — Starter, $2,499:** booking page with your branding, your
> rooms and rates. You look professional, clients can reach you.
>
> **Option 2 — Pro, $3,499:** everything in Starter, plus the full room +
> song booking flow, the live estimate engine, and session sheet downloads.
>
> **Option 3 — Premium, $3,999:** everything in Pro, plus review
> automation and Google Business setup so the sessions start finding you."

### STAGE 5 — COMMITMENT

> "Do you feel like this could be the answer for you?"

> "Why do you feel like it would, though?" *(Let them sell themselves. Do
> not interrupt. This is the most important question on the call.)*

> "What specific parts do you feel are gonna help you the most?"

> "Which one of those would you lean towards?" → "How come that one, just
> so I understand?"

**Funding — say funding, never budget:**
> "Okay — so the funding you'd need for us to build this out and get you
> to [their want] would be [price]. Any questions on how that works?"

**Next step:**
> "Really, the next step is we lock in your build slot — I send over a
> short form for your logo, rooms, and rates, and we get your site live
> within [X days]. Would that be appropriate, or how... would you like to
> proceed from here?"

**Send the checkout link for the package and funding option they chose.**
Deposit is 50% on the checkout page. The balance is only due when the site
ships, and only once they are happy with it.

## PART 3 — OBJECTIONS (never rebut — ask questions)

**"I need to think about it."**
> "Totally fair. Can I ask — what specifically do you feel you need to
> think over? Is it whether this would actually get you those extra
> sessions... or is it more the funding side?"
*(Whatever they say is the real concern. Go back to questions on that — do
not pitch at it.)*

**"It's too expensive."**
> "Money aside — do you feel like this is something that would work for
> you?"
*(Get the yes first. Then:)*
> "Tell me — if you did have the funding, is this something that would work
> for you?"
> "What other avenues do you have to find the funds so you can [their
> want]?"

**"Just send me some info."**
> "Yeah, I can do that for sure — just so I send the right thing, what
> exactly are you looking for?"
*(Then: "Got it — and who do you use now for...?" Back into situation
questions. Never send info without a next step: "Let's suppose you go
through it and it fits — what would you want the next step to be?")*

**"I already have a website."**
> "Oh nice — what do you like about it?"
> "What would you change about it if you could, though?"
> "Can clients actually book a session on it step-by-step... or does it
> mostly just... sit there?"

**"Let me talk to my partner."**
> "Of course — can you walk me through how you two usually decide on
> something like this?"
> "What do you think they'd ask about? ... Let's pretend they love it —
> what do you see happening next?"

**"How do I know this will work?"**
> "That's fair — what specifically would you want to see to feel good about
> it?"

## PART 4 — CHEAT SHEET (tape to your monitor)

- **85% questions, 10% presenting, 5% closing.** If you are talking, you
  are losing.
- They persuade themselves or it does not stick. Your job is questions +
  silence.
- Verbal pauses... slow down... concerned tone on pain, challenging tone on
  consequence.
- Never present before the gap is built. No gap = "I need to think about
  it."
- Turn every statement into a question.
- "Money aside, do you feel like this would work for you?" — get the yes
  before funding.
- Say **funding**, never **budget**.
- Downplay the win (mismatching) — let them inflate it.
- End every call with a calendar commitment. Never "I'll follow up."
- Roleplay this out loud 5 times before your first real call. It will feel
  weird. That is the point.
